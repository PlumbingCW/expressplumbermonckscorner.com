Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation in Moncks Corner, SC

A garbage disposal grinds food scraps so they can wash down the drain. When it jams, leaks or stops, the kitchen sink backs up quickly. Turn the switch off first and never put your hand inside the unit. If water is pouring out, close the shutoff valves under the sink and call.

A jammed unit often has a hex socket on the bottom for a small Allen wrench. Turn it back and forth to free the flywheel, then press the red reset button. If the unit trips again at once, the motor has failed.

What Should Never Go Down a Disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the pipe downstream.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts, which pack into a sludge.
  • Potato peels, pasta and rice, which swell into paste.
  • Fibrous vegetables such as celery and corn husks.
  • Bones, fruit pits and non-food objects.

Even a strong disposal depends on the pipe after it. Grease is the most common cause of a kitchen line that clogs again and again.

How Long Does It Take to Install a Garbage Disposal?

A straight swap of a similar unit often takes about an hour. A first-time install needs a power outlet or wiring, a switch and a dishwasher connection, so plan for longer.

Dishwasher Drain and Local Considerations

The dishwasher drains through the disposal in most kitchens. A knockout plug inside the new unit must be removed, or the dishwasher will not drain. Many Moncks Corner homes with septic systems have extra concerns, so tell the plumber if you are on septic, because ground food scraps load a septic tank faster than most people expect.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

Disposal drains and dishwasher connections follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including the air gap or high loop. Wiring follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), so ask about a switched outlet at the visit.
