Plumbing Installation in Moncks Corner, SC

Plumbing installation covers new fixtures, new supply and drain lines, and connecting appliances. It is planned work, from replacing a vanity to adding a bathroom. For an active leak or backup, call the 24/7 line for repair instead.

Berkeley County has seen steady building, and the town grew quickly in the last decade. That keeps remodels and additions busy. Each one needs plumbing that fits the existing home.

What We Install

Kitchen

Sinks, faucets, disposals, dishwasher lines and pot fillers.

Bathroom

Toilets, vanities, tubs, showers and valves.

Laundry

Washer boxes, drain standpipes and utility sinks.

Outdoor

Hose bibs, outdoor sinks and irrigation tie-ins.

Water heaters

See water heater installation.

Whole house

Shutoff valves, pressure reducing valves and filtration hookups.

Adding a fixture often means adding a vent and a drain slope. A plumber checks the layout before you buy the fixture, so you do not buy something that cannot connect.

Slab Homes and Pier-and-Beam Homes

Slab homes need drains cut into concrete for a new bathroom, so planning the location matters. Homes with a crawlspace allow easier access but need insulation against humidity. A plumber who has seen both can tell you which route costs less for your plan.

Kitchens, Disposals and Appliances

A new dishwasher needs a supply, a drain connection and an air gap or high loop. A new refrigerator ice maker needs a supply line with a shutoff. Replacing a leaking supply hose with a braided steel line is a small job that prevents a large flood. See garbage disposal installation for the sink side.

Getting Ready for an Installation Visit

  • Clear the work area and the path to it.
  • Locate the main water shutoff and the breaker for the area.
  • Have fixture boxes and manuals on hand.
  • Photograph the existing plumbing, if you can.
  • Decide who handles drywall, tile and paint afterward.

A plumber can only plan around what is visible. The more you can show, the closer the first price will be to the final one.

Choosing Fixtures That Last

Look for fixtures with metal valve bodies and ceramic cartridges, which hold up better than plastic. Check that replacement parts are easy to find, because a stylish faucet with discontinued parts becomes a full replacement the first time something wears out. Ask the plumber which brands they find easy to service.

Water-saving ratings matter too. Low-flow showerheads and toilets can lower your water bill, but some feel weaker than others. If you can, test one in a showroom before you commit.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

New plumbing is built to the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted and amended locally, with the Town of Moncks Corner Building Department as the authority in town. Wiring for appliances follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).
