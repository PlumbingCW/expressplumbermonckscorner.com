Causes of Clogged Drains in Moncks Corner, SC and How to Prevent Them

Most clogs start small. Hair, grease, paper and soap add up, and a rough or damaged pipe holds on to them.

Here are the common causes, what each looks like and how to stop it.

Cause One: Grease and Fat

Grease is liquid when it leaves the pan and solid when it cools in the pipe. It coats the wall and catches other debris. Pouring hot water after it does not help, since the grease moves down the line and hardens farther along.

Wipe pans with a paper towel and throw it away. Pour cooled grease into a can for the trash.

Cause Two: Hair and Soap

In showers and bathroom sinks, hair tangles around the stopper and in the trap. Soap scum glues it together. A hair catcher in every drain is the cheapest prevention.

Cause Three: Wipes and Paper Products

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like toilet paper. Paper towels, feminine products and cotton swabs do not either. They catch on rough spots and build up. Only toilet paper belongs in a toilet.

Cause Four: Food Scraps

Coffee grounds, rice, pasta and potato peels swell or clump. A disposal helps with soft scraps, but it does not make the line forgiving. Scrape plates into the trash first.

Cause Five: Mineral Scale

In hard water, minerals deposit on the inside of pipes and narrow them over years. Scale also catches debris. If you see white crust on faucets and showerheads, your pipes likely have scale too. Read your utility's annual water quality report or use a test strip to see your mineral level.

Cause Six: Tree Roots

Roots seek moisture and nutrients and find them in tiny cracks at pipe joints. Once inside, they spread like a net and catch paper. Roots are a main line problem, and a camera shows them. Cutting them is temporary unless the joint is repaired. Our hydro jetting page explains how roots and scale are removed.

Cause Seven: Damaged or Sagging Pipe

A cracked, offset or bellied pipe catches solids at the same spot again and again. A cable clears it, but it returns. That is when a camera check and a pipe repair are the right fix.

Cause Eight: Foreign Objects

Toys, jewelry, dental floss and other small items end up in drains. They snag paper and grease and build a plug. A plumber can retrieve many with a cable or by pulling a toilet.

Moncks Corner Notes

Moncks Corner gets about 52 inches of rain a year, and the town sits only about 33 feet above sea level. Soil stays wet much of the year, which feeds roots and loosens joints. Older homes near the downtown core often have clay or cast iron lines, while newer neighborhoods use PVC.

Berkeley County Water and Sanitation and the Moncks Corner Public Works Commission each manage parts of the area's water and sewer systems. If a clog is in the street main, the utility handles it. A camera and a distance measurement help show which side of the property line the problem is on.

Habits That Prevent Most Clogs

  • Install hair catchers in every shower and tub.
  • Wipe, do not rinse, greasy pans.
  • Flush only toilet paper.
  • Run hot water after dishes.
  • Keep the cleanout accessible.
  • Have the main line camera-checked if it clogs more than once a year.

How to Tell Which Cause You Have

The location of the slow drain is the best clue. One slow bathroom sink is almost always hair and soap in the trap. A slow kitchen sink that smells like old food is grease. A toilet and a shower that slow down together point to a shared branch. Every fixture slowing down at once points to the main line.

Timing is another clue. A clog that appears suddenly, right after something went down the drain, is usually a foreign object or a clump of paper. A clog that builds over weeks is buildup. A clog that returns every few weeks, however many times it is cleared, is a pipe condition such as roots or a sag.

What a Plumber Does That a Plunger Cannot

A plunger moves water back and forth. A cable cuts or hooks the clog. A camera shows what the pipe looks like and why the clog formed. Hydro jetting washes the whole wall. Matching the tool to the cause saves money, because the wrong tool clears the symptom and leaves the cause behind.

If you have cleared the same drain three times this year, stop buying drain opener and ask for a camera. The answer is usually cheaper than another year of clogs.

When Prevention Is Not Enough

Even careful households get clogs, because pipes age and roots grow. If a drain clogs more than twice a year, treat that as a signal and not bad luck. A camera inspection costs less than repeated emergency calls, and it tells you whether a repair, a jetting or a change in habits is the right next step. Standards and Sources

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets minimum drain sizes and slopes, and a pipe that falls short of that standard clogs more often.
