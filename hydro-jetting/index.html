Hydro Jetting in Moncks Corner: Scrubbing the Pipe, Not Just Poking It

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water hose with a rotating nozzle to blast grease, scale, silt and root fibers off the inside of a pipe. Where a cable bores a hole through a blockage, a jetter cleans the whole wall. If your main line backs up in Moncks Corner at night, call the 24/7 line and a plumber can be sent.

While you wait for a backup, stop running water, keep the toilets unflushed and move belongings off the floor near the lowest drain. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

When a Main Line Backup Is an Emergency

  • Several drains back up together or sewage appears in a tub or floor drain.
  • A cable machine got stuck or stopped short of the main sewer connection.
  • A camera shows roots, grease or scale narrowing the pipe.
  • The same line has needed clearing more than twice in a year.
  • A restaurant or business drain slows during peak hours.

A first-time clog in one sink rarely needs jetting. A pattern does. If you are on your third service call for the same line, ask for a camera and a jetting quote instead of another cable visit.

How Hydro Jetting Works

1

Camera first

A video run shows pipe condition, roots and any breaks. Jetting a cracked pipe can make it worse.

2

Set up and jet

A hose with a nozzle runs through the cleanout. Water pressure spins the nozzle and scours the wall.

3

Flush and recover

Loosened debris washes downstream or is recovered at the cleanout.

4

Camera again

A second pass confirms the pipe is clear and the walls are clean.

Most residential jetting is done through a cleanout in the yard or a pulled toilet. If you cannot find your cleanout, tell the dispatcher, because locating it can add time to the visit.

Roots, Grease and Scale in Berkeley County Lines

Moncks Corner gets about 52 inches of rain a year and sits close to the Cooper River, so soil stays wet and tree roots reach for the moisture inside sewer joints. Fine roots then catch toilet paper and grease. Jetting clears fiber roots, but heavy roots need a cutting head first, and a damaged joint needs repair.

Restaurants along US 52 and Main Street face grease on top of roots. Jetting grease lines is routine work, but only part of the job. A grease trap that is pumped on schedule keeps jetting from becoming a monthly expense. See our commercial plumbing page for after-hours support.

When Not to Jet

High-pressure water is not for every pipe. Cracked, collapsed or bellied pipe can be damaged or can hide more of the problem. Old clay and fragile cast iron also need care. That is why the camera comes first, and why we recommend sewer line repair when the camera shows a break instead of jetting through it.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

Before any digging near a cleanout or lateral, South Carolina residents are urged to call 811 so buried utilities are marked. Sewer work also follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for cleanout access and pipe slope.

What to Do While You Wait

  • Keep toilets unflushed.
  • Move belongings away from the lowest drain.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing wastewater.
  • Take photos for your insurer before any cleanup.
