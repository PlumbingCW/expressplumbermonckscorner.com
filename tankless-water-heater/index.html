Tankless Water Heater Service in Moncks Corner, SC

A tankless water heater heats water only as it flows through the unit, so it does not store a tank of hot water. Service in Moncks Corner covers new installs, repairs, error-code diagnosis, and the yearly flush that keeps the heat exchanger clean.

People call about tankless units for three reasons. They want to switch from a tank, a unit is showing an error code, or an older unit has stopped keeping up. Each needs a different visit, and the plumber tells you which as soon as you describe the problem.

Before You Install: Gas Line, Vent and Flow

  • Gas line size: tankless burners draw more gas at once than a tank, so a line that fed a tank may be too small.
  • Venting: most gas tankless models need a sealed or direct vent through an exterior wall or roof.
  • Flow rate: the unit's gallons per minute must cover the fixtures you run together.
  • Temperature rise: colder incoming water means a bigger rise and lower flow.
  • Electric supply: even gas units need an outlet, and electric units need heavy circuits.

A gas tankless heater is the same appliance whether it is called a whole-house unit or an on-demand heater. Size the unit for the demand you actually have, not the largest unit on the shelf.

Flush and Descale: The Maintenance Callers Ask About Most

Minerals in the water gradually coat the heat exchanger, restricting flow and raising operating temperature until the unit throws an error. Manufacturers generally recommend a flush with descaling solution once a year, and sooner if your water has high mineral content. Check your manual for the interval it sets.

A flush uses a small pump and two hoses to circulate a mild acid through the heat exchanger, then rinses it. It takes about an hour and restores flow. Skipping it is the most common reason a tankless unit fails early.

Hard Water, Soft Water and What We Do Not Guess

Use that report or a store-bought test strip to see your numbers. If the mineral level is high, a water softener or scale-prevention cartridge may be worth pricing alongside the tankless unit.

Repairs and Error Codes

Most tankless failures have a code. An ignition code can mean low gas pressure, a dirty flame rod or a failed igniter. A venting code usually means a blocked exhaust or intake. We read the code, check the manual, test gas pressure and fix the cause rather than clearing the code and hoping.

If a unit is more than a decade old and parts are scarce, replacement may be wiser than another repair. That is also true when a tank water heater has failed and you are weighing a switch. In those cases water heater replacement is the place to compare tank and tankless options.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

Tankless units are installed to the manufacturer's venting and gas instructions and to the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54). The same manuals set the flush interval we follow, and the U.S. Department of Energy suggests 120 degrees as a starting temperature setting.
