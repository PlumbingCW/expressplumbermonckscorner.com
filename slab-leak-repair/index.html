Slab Leak Repair in Moncks Corner, SC

A slab leak is a leak in a pressurized water line that runs under or through the concrete foundation. Because the pipe is buried, the signs are indirect. If you suspect one, shut off the main valve and call our 24/7 line. A plumber can be sent to your Moncks Corner address.

Check the water meter after closing the main valve at the house. If the dial still moves, the leak is outside the house or under it. A plumber can then separate the yard line from the slab lines with a pressure test.

Signs of a Slab Leak

  • A warm or hot spot on a tile, wood or concrete floor.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • Damp carpet, lifting flooring or cracks in tile along a line.
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use.
  • Low water pressure, or a water heater that runs nonstop.

A hot-water line leaking under the slab is the classic case, because you feel it as warmth. Cold-water slab leaks are quieter and often reveal themselves only through the meter and the bill.

Why Slab Leaks Happen Here

Berkeley County soil holds moisture, and Moncks Corner gets about 52 inches of rain a year. Pipes under a slab can corrode where they touch concrete or soil, and shifting ground rubs a line against rock or the slab itself. In some homes, copper lines under the slab pit from the outside, while plastic lines can fail at fittings.

The oldest slab homes in town are not the only ones affected. A fast-growing area also has homes where pipe was installed quickly, and a single bad fitting under a slab can leak years later. A leak test is the only reliable answer, not the age of the house.

Repair Options: Spot Repair, Reroute or Repipe

Spot repair through the slab

  • The leak is a single point
  • Pipe elsewhere is in good condition
  • Floor can be opened and patched
  • Lowest scope of work

Reroute or repipe

  • Several leaks or corroded pipe
  • The slab should not be cut
  • New lines run through walls or attic
  • More work, but avoids future slab leaks

A reroute abandons the slab line and runs a new one overhead through the attic or walls. For homes with repeat failures, repiping can cost more up front and save the floor from future cuts. The plumber explains each path and its cost, and you choose.

Insurance, Flooring and Drying

Some homeowner policies cover access and repair when a slab leak causes sudden damage, though wear-related leaks are often excluded. Ask your agent before work starts. Wet flooring and underlayment dry slowly in humid weather, so ask the plumber about drying before new floors go in.
