Local Plumbing Notes

What Moncks Corner Homes Ask of Their Plumbing

Heavy rain and low ground

Moncks Corner averages about 52 inches of rain a year, and the town sits about 33 feet above sea level near the West Branch of the Cooper River. After a long downpour, a slow main drain or a gurgling floor drain is often the first warning. If the yard stays soggy, ask us to camera the line before a backup reaches the house.

A fast-growing town

Town officials expected the 2020 Census to show roughly 60 percent growth since 2010, from about 8,000 residents to about 13,000. Newer subdivisions mean newer pipe, while older homes near downtown and Main Street may still have decades-old galvanized or cast iron lines, which are worth inspecting before a leak shows up.

Cold snaps are rare, not unheard of

The town's record low is 11 degrees Fahrenheit. Most winters never test pipes, but one hard freeze can split an exposed hose bib or an unheated crawlspace line. Insulating outdoor spigots in November costs far less than a burst pipe in January.

Know your water utility

The Moncks Corner Public Works Commission publishes an annual water quality report, as does the City of Goose Creek. We do not guess at hardness numbers. Read your utility's report or test your tap before you choose a softener, and ask us how mineral buildup affects your water heater.
