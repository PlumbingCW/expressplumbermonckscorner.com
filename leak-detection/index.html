Leak Detection in Moncks Corner, SC: Find It Before You Fix It

Leak detection is the process of locating a hidden water leak using meter tests, pressure tests, listening equipment, tracer gas and thermal imaging, so only the leaking section of pipe gets opened. If water is actively running, call our 24/7 line and a plumber can be sent to your Moncks Corner address.

Start by shutting off the main water valve. If the meter dial keeps turning with every fixture off, the leak is between the meter and the house or inside it. Take a photo of the meter reading and the time. That simple record helps the plumber and your water utility.

Meter Still Running? High Water Bill? Start Here

Many leak calls begin with a bill that is two or three times normal, or a meter that moves when nobody is home. Turn everything off, including ice makers and irrigation, and watch the meter's low-flow indicator. A spinning triangle or moving dial confirms water is leaving the pipe.

Next, close the main valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house, the so-called service line. That buried pipe is usually the homeowner's responsibility to repair, and our water service line repair page explains what the repair involves.

How We Pinpoint Leaks Without Tearing Things Up

  • Pressure testing: sections of pipe are isolated and watched for pressure loss.
  • Acoustic listening: sensitive microphones pick up the hiss of leaking pressurized pipe through floors and soil.
  • Thermal imaging: a thermal camera shows temperature differences from hot-water leaks or wet materials.
  • Tracer gas: a safe mix of nitrogen and hydrogen is pushed into drained pipe, and a sniffer finds where it escapes.
  • Moisture mapping: meters trace wet drywall back to the source.

Tracer gas methods, sometimes called helium or hydrogen leak testing, find leaks too small to hear. We use the method that matches your situation, because no single tool works for every leak.

Ceiling, Wall, Under-House and Crawlspace Leaks

Not every leak is under concrete. A stain on a ceiling can come from a drain line, a supply line or an air-conditioning condensate line, and water can travel a long way along a joist before it drips. Under-house and crawlspace leaks hide under insulation and show up as mold, a musty smell or higher humidity.

Berkeley County humidity makes crawlspace leaks easy to miss, since damp air already hangs under many homes. If your floors feel soft, your humidity stays high or you smell mildew, ask for a crawlspace check. A short look under the house is much cheaper than replacing a floor.

Yard, Irrigation and Pool Leaks

Wet patches in the yard, a lawn that is greener in a stripe or a sprinkler zone that runs weak can mean a leaking irrigation or service line. Pool and spa lines hide leaks of their own. We handle the plumbing side of yard leaks and can locate buried lines, then repair what we find.

Call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked first. We follow that rule on every excavation.

Why Hidden Leaks Matter More Around Moncks Corner

Moncks Corner averages about 52 inches of rain, and soil near the Cooper River stays moist much of the year. A buried leak softens the ground around it, which can hide the surface signs. Meanwhile many town neighborhoods hold homes of different ages, from older downtown houses to subdivisions built after the town's rapid growth. Older homes tend to have galvanized or copper pipe nearing the end of its life.
