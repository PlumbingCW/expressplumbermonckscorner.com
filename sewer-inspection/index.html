Sewer Camera Inspection in Moncks Corner, SC

A sewer camera inspection feeds a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the cleanout or a pulled toilet, and records the pipe interior as it goes. The plumber watches live for cracks, offsets, roots, grease and sags, and notes the distance to each problem.

The result is video and a plain-language summary, not a guess. For backups and repeated clogs, it tells us whether a cable, a jet or a repair is the right answer. For home purchases, it tells you what you would own before closing.

When to Order an Inspection

Repeating backups

If cables fix the clog and it returns, the camera shows why.

Buying a home

Older lateral pipe is a common surprise on inspection reports.

After a repair

A second pass proves the line is clear and the work is sound.

Before landscaping

Roots and pavers can load a line. Check before adding a patio.

Planned inspections do not need an emergency visit, but if sewage is already backing up, call the 24/7 line. We can clear the line and run the camera in the same visit when time allows. Our drain cleaning page explains the clearing side.

Pipe Materials in Berkeley County Homes

Pipe material tells us what to look for. Older homes near the Moncks Corner downtown area may have clay or cast iron laterals. Cast iron corrodes from the inside and flakes. Clay cracks at joints and lets roots in. Newer neighborhoods use PVC, which resists roots but can sag where backfill settled.

Berkeley County's wet ground and the town's recent growth bring both kinds of homes into the same market. When a house changes hands, a camera is the best way to find which type it has and what shape it is in. Keep the recording with your closing documents.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

Camera inspections follow the equipment maker's guidance, and the findings are measured against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) rules for slope and cleanouts. A recording is the record you keep for a sale or an insurance claim.
