Foxbank Plantation, Moncks Corner, SC

Foxbank Plantation is a planned residential community at the corner of US 52 and Cypress Gardens Road, with a Moncks Corner address in ZIP code 29461. A 2018 announcement placed it between Goose Creek and Moncks Corner, so it is one of the first neighborhoods you reach driving north on US 52. Street names in the community include Foxbank Plantation Boulevard, Trotters Lane, Red Leaf Boulevard and Recess Pointe Drive.

The name comes from an older rice plantation. A state plantation history site records a 430-acre tract sold in 1819 and says the land was being developed into a planned housing community by 2005. We do not know the exact boundary of the community today, so we describe it by its anchor roads and do not draw a line on a map.

Public Facts About the Area

A school on donated land

The developer gave land to the Berkeley County School District for Foxbank Elementary, set to open for the 2018 to 2019 school year with about 750 students.

A fire station site

About one acre beside the school was transferred to the Town of Moncks Corner for a fire station, according to the same 2018 report.

Mixed home types

Listings in Foxbank Plantation include both houses and townhomes. The type of building decides who is responsible for a given pipe.

Shops on US 52

A commercial project called Foxbank Towne Center is marketed on US 52 beside the community, so errands are close by.

Houses and Townhomes: Who Owns Which Pipe

In a detached house, the plumbing from the water meter to your fixtures, and the sewer line from the house to the connection at the street, is normally the owner's responsibility. In a townhome, a shared wall can mean shared drain stacks or a shared sewer lateral. That is general plumbing information, not a statement about any one building in Foxbank.

Before you pay for work on a line that may serve more than one unit, read your association documents and ask the association. It is also worth asking the Moncks Corner building department whether a permit applies to the work.

Practical Steps for a Planned Community Home

  • Find your water meter and main shutoff before you need them, and show everyone in the house.
  • Ask your association whether outdoor spigots, irrigation and shared lines are its job or yours.
  • Read the label on your water heater for its age. A tank past ten years old deserves a closer look.
  • Test your tap: if hot water runs out sooner than it used to, say so when you call.
  • Keep a photo of any leak and the date you noticed it, which helps an insurer or an association.
