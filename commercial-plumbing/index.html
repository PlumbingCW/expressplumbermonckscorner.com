Commercial Plumbing in Moncks Corner, SC, Day or Night

Commercial plumbing covers the larger drains, heaters, restrooms and kitchens of businesses and rental properties. If a restaurant drain backs up or a restroom floods after closing, call our 24/7 line and a plumber can be sent to your Moncks Corner address.

While you wait, shut off the water to the affected area, stop using sinks and toilets that share the line, and keep customers and staff away from standing water. If sewage is involved, keep the area clear and do not clean it up without protection.

Restaurants and Grease

Kitchens along US 52 and Main Street put heavy loads of grease and food solids into their lines. Grease cools in the pipe and builds a wall that a cable only pokes through. Jetting scours the wall. A grease trap that is cleaned on schedule prevents most repeat backups. We do not clean traps, but we can tell you if yours is part of the problem.

Backups, Backflow and Floor Drains

Floor drains in kitchens, mechanical rooms and restrooms can back up when the main is blocked. Backflow preventers protect the public water supply from your building's plumbing. Testing rules for backflow preventers are set by the utility and the state, so ask your water provider about requirements. We can repair or replace the device and discuss the testing process.

Property Managers and Rentals

Property managers need fast answers and clear paperwork. We note what was found and what was done at each visit, so you can pass it to the owner. Repeat clogs in the same unit are worth a camera check. See sewer inspection. Tenant-caused damage and building problems are different, and photos help sort which is which.

Scheduled Work and Upgrades

Not every commercial call is an emergency. Planned work includes restroom remodels, water heater upgrades, line replacement and new fixtures, and these are best scheduled outside business hours. See plumbing installation for how new work is planned and priced.

What to Have Ready When You Call

  • The business name and address, and where in the building the problem is.
  • Whether the water is off and which fixtures are affected.
  • Whether sewage is involved.
  • A contact who can give access after hours.
  • The location of the main water shutoff and the cleanouts, if you know them.

A clear description speeds up dispatch and helps the plumber bring the right equipment. If you do not know the answers, say so. The plumber can find them on arrival.

Codes and Standards Behind This Work

Commercial work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted locally, and backflow device testing is set by your water provider and the state. Ask both which rules apply to your building.
